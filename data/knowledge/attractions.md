# HCMC Attractions & Experiences Guide

## Chợ Bến Thành (Ben Thanh Market)
Chợ Bến Thành is Ho Chi Minh City's most iconic central market, located in District 1 at Quách Thị Trang Square. Built in 1914, its south entrance clock tower is a universally recognized symbol of the city. Inside, over 1,500 stalls sell textiles, clothing, lacquerware, coffee beans, dried tropical fruits, and souvenirs. In the evening, the surrounding streets transform into an open-air night market packed with seafood grills, stir-fry vendors, and clothing bargains. Bargaining is standard practice for non-food items; aim for 30% to 50% off initial quoted prices politely.

## Phố Đi Bộ Nguyễn Huệ (Nguyen Hue Walking Street)
Nguyen Hue is a grand 670-meter pedestrian boulevard stretching from the French colonial Ho Chi Minh City Hall (People's Committee Building) to the Saigon River bank. Lined with modern skyscrapers, historic apartment cafes (notably The Cafe Apartments at 42 Nguyen Hue), and interactive fountains, it is the premier gathering spot in the evening. Locals and travelers come here to socialize, watch street performers, skateboard, and sip peach lemongrass tea.

## Bảo Tàng Chứng Tích Chiến Tranh (War Remnants Museum)
Located on Vo Van Tan Street in District 3, the War Remnants Museum is one of Vietnam's most visited museums. It offers powerful, sobering documentation of the Vietnam War and the First Indochina War. Key exhibits include outdoor displays of captured U.S. military aircraft, artillery, and tanks; the Requiem photojournalism gallery commemorating photographers killed in combat; and exhibits documenting the humanitarian consequences of Agent Orange and unexploded ordnance. Open daily from 7:30 AM to 5:30 PM.

## Chợ Lớn & Chùa Bà Thiên Hậu (District 5 Chinatown)
Chợ Lớn encompasses Districts 5 and 6 and is home to the largest ethnic Chinese community in Vietnam. Highlights include Chợ Bình Tây (Binh Tay Wholesale Market) with its two-story yellow roof and central courtyard, and Chùa Bà Thiên Hậu (Thien Hau Temple) on Nguyen Trai Street. Built in 1760 by the Cantonese congregation, the temple honors Mazu, the goddess of sea travelers. It is famous for intricate porcelain roof figurines and massive conical incense spirals hanging from the rafters that burn for weeks.
