# HCMC Food & Culinary Guide

## Cơm Tấm (Broken Rice)
Cơm tấm is the quintessential Ho Chi Minh City dish, originally made from fractured rice grains left over from milling. It is traditionally served with sườn nướng (marinated grilled pork chops charred over hot coals), bì (shredded pork skin dusted with roasted rice powder), chả trứng (steamed pork and egg meatloaf with egg yolk topping), and a sunny-side-up fried egg (trứng ốp la). The dish is garnished with mỡ hành (scallion oil), dưa chua (pickled carrots and daikon), cucumber slices, and served with a small bowl of sweet, garlicky, and slightly spicy fish sauce (nước mắm chua ngọt). Iconic places to eat include Cơm Tấm Ba Ghiền (Dang Van Ngu Street, Phu Nhuan District) and Cơm Tấm Thuận Kiều. It is eaten for breakfast, lunch, dinner, or late-night supper.

## Phở Sài Gòn (Southern Beef Noodle Soup)
Unlike Northern Pho which emphasizes a clear, delicate, and simple broth, Southern Phở Sài Gòn features a richer, slightly sweeter broth flavored with charred onions, ginger, star anise, cinnamon, and rock sugar. It is served with an abundant herb platter including ngò gai (sawtooth herb), húng quế (Thai basil), bean sprouts (giá), and lime wedges. Diners customize their bowls with tương đen (sweet fermented bean paste/hoisin) and tương ớt (sriracha chili sauce). Cuts of beef include tái (rare beef slices), nạm (flank), gầu (fatty brisket), and bò viên (beef meatballs). Famous historic establishments include Phở Hòa Pasteur (Pasteur Street, District 3) and Phở Lệ (Nguyen Trai Street, District 5).

## Bánh Mì Sài Gòn (Vietnamese Baguette)
Bánh mì in Saigon is a crispy, airy French baguette split open and filled with an assortment of savory ingredients. Classic fillings include chả lụa (pork roll), thịt nguội (cured ham), rich liver pâté (pate gan), homemade egg yolk mayonnaise (bơ), pickled daikon and carrot, fresh cucumber batons, cilantro sprigs, and fiery bird's eye chilies. It is finished with a dash of seasoning sauce (nước tương/Maggi). Popular vendors include Bánh Mì Huỳnh Hoa (Le Thi Rieng, District 1) known for massive meat-packed loaves, Bánh Mì Bảy Hổ, and neighborhood street carts across every corner of the city.

## Bún Riêu Cua (Crab and Tomato Noodle Soup)
Bún Riêu Cua is a tangy, savory noodle soup featuring a broth made from simmered pork bones, ripe tomatoes, and pounded freshwater paddy crabs (cua đồng). The star of the dish is the riêu cua—tender, cloud-like patties of crab meat, shrimp paste, and beaten eggs floating in the broth. Additional toppings include fried tofu cubes (đậu hũ chiên), pork blood curd (huyết), and Vietnamese pork sausage. It is served with fresh greens like shredded morning glory (rau muống chẻ), banana blossoms (bắp chuối), and a spoonful of pungent shrimp paste (mắm tôm) for extra umami. A renowned spot is Bún Riêu Gánh near Ben Thanh Market.

## Cà Phê Vợt & Cà Phê Sữa Đá (Saigon Coffee Culture)
Coffee in Saigon is a cultural ritual. Cà phê sữa đá is strong robusta dark roast brewed through a slow metal drip filter (phin) directly over thick sweetened condensed milk, then poured over crushed ice. Another heritage method is cà phê vợt (cloth net-filtered coffee), where coffee grounds are brewed in a fabric sock filter inside clay pots over charcoal stoves, creating a smooth and smoky aroma. Famous 24/7 heritage stalls include Cà Phê Vợt Ba Lù (Chợ Phùng Hưng, District 5) and Cà Phê Vợt Che Leo (Nguyen Thien Thuat, District 3, operating since 1938).
